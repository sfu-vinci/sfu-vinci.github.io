**Talk Title**: Understanding and designing around error in interactive systems

**Abstract**:  
In Human-Computer Interaction, "errors" are often seen as inappropriate and preventable actions on the part of the user, such as typing mistakes, clicking on buttons by accident, or selecting the incorrect command. This view makes them easy to measure and evaluate, but oversimplifies their definition, the analysis of the causes of undesirable computer responses, and their real consequences for people. By studying the use of the term in different fields of research (HCI, ergonomics, safety science…) and the way people experience errors, Dr. Loizeau broadened this definition by centering it on subjective experience, namely the cost and responsibility felt. This allowed the introduction of new characterizations of interface errors, including notions of guilt, frustration, or physiological limits that are typically disregarded. It also shed light on phenomena that are negative experiences for the user but would not typically be considered errors in classical conventions, such as interaction interferences and slips.

Her work allows for a better understanding of the causes and consequences of errors, both from the human point of view and from that of the system or its design, allowing her to create, implement and evaluate new design rules for interactive systems, taking better account of each user's capabilities, perceptions and intentions.

**Biography**:  
Dr. Alice Loizeau is a researcher in Human-Computer Interaction. She holds two master's degrees in computer engineering and UX design from the University of Technology of Compiègne (UTC, France) and a bachelor in Humanities and Technology from the same school. She completed her PhD in December 2025 at the Inria center of the University of Lille (France), where she was supervised by Mathieu Nancel and Stéphane Huot. She is currently working as a Post-Doctoral Fellow with Carl Gutwin in the Interaction Lab of the University of Saskatchewan.
